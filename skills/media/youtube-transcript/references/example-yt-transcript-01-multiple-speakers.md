## Summary
Guest Mira Kelso joins Riverbend Conversations to debate whether "smart notebooks" are merely fancy stationery or something that could reshape how people capture ideas. The hosts open with Kelso's odd path into product design — building cardboard prototypes as a teenager, skipping a conventional design degree, and shipping a paper-plus-ink gadget by age 26 — then pivot to whether schools should still teach handwriting when every desk has a tablet.

The disagreement centers on "paperism": the claim that notebooks are inert objects with no goals of their own, versus Kelso's view that sync features, suggestion chips, and always-on capture already make devices behave as if they have agendas. She defends a narrow thesis that any task with clear rules and lots of practice examples (spelling drills, meeting notes) will eventually be matched by the gadgets, while remaining unsure about open-ended work like mentoring or improvising a joke.

The hosts push back with data-exhaustion arguments and a claim that humans invent questions before collecting evidence, while gadgets only ingest. Kelso calls that a "confusion of levels," notes she spent two years at a fictional stationery lab, and ends with bets about whether a device will ever write a funny one-hour standup special without human cherry-picking.
