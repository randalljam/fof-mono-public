Why Fable 5 and not Opus 4.8 (the short version)

Opus 4.8 is excellent and half the price — it's the right tool for well-scoped implementation and mechanical edits, and you should keep using it for those. Fable 5's edge is exactly where these five missions live: long-horizon autonomous runs, first-shot builds of well-specified systems, navigating ambiguity, parallel sub-agent delegation, and self-verification. On that kind of work a lesser model produces shallow plans or subtly-wrong scaffolding that you then have to catch — the opposite of what you want. Reserve Fable's pricier, capped budget for the tasks where its marginal intelligence changes what you get, and let it hand the mechanical parts to Opus.

01 · How it's built

Modular by design — short prompts, shared context

Fable 5's own guidance warns that prompts written for older models are too prescriptive and lower its output quality. So these prompts are short: they state a goal and its boundaries and defer everything else to two enduring documents. That keeps each prompt small, keeps the rules consistent across all five, and means you tune the whole system by editing one doc instead of five prompts.

context brief

Who Randy is, the repo shape, the harness landscape, the current gaps, the hard boundaries. Orientation a fresh session can trust.

operating contract

Pace, autonomy defaults, verification, what every mission hands back (incl. the phone-friendly approval packet).

+ one short prompt

Each mission = goal + its specific boundaries + "operate under the two docs." That's it.

= a reviewable PR

A branch, tests, a plan doc, and an approval packet you can act on from your phone.

The pace guardrail (so you don't get buried)

The operating contract's first rule is pace: one mission → one branch → one coherent PR, additive over destructive, stop at the natural checkpoint, no unrequested refactors or "tidying." The prompts default to stopping at "branch pushed + approval packet" rather than opening PRs — nothing merges or deploys without you. That's what keeps "smarter and more autonomous" from turning into "more cleanup for Randy."

03 · The missions

The five prompts

Each links to its full copy-paste file. The two boxes under each are the TLDR you asked for: why Fable 5 (not Opus 4.8) and what it accomplishes. Weight and a rough token estimate are on the right; the budget section explains them.

1 Portfolio Cartographer

Read everything — all apps, core, and the nine branches — and produce one decision-ready map: a refreshed PROJECTS.md, a prioritized top-10 next-actions roadmap, a dependency/sequencing view, and a risk register. Documentation only; zero code risk.

Why Fable 5, not Opus 4.8

Prioritizing across a whole portfolio means holding ~20 projects in mind at once and making leverage-to-effort judgment calls. A weaker model gives a plausible-looking but shallow list you'd have to re-do — here the synthesis quality is the deliverable.

What it accomplishes

Turns a sprawling repo into a single, honest, ranked plan of what to do next and in what order — the "get organized" outcome, with the highest-value moves surfaced.

weight: medium ~3.5M tok · ~$60 open prompt →

2 Safety Rails — CI & test floor

Stand up the minimum CI: a GitHub Actions workflow that runs the offline-safe tests on PRs, with the real-API tests cleanly separated and skipped in CI, plus a runbook proposing the next two layers (AI review, self-verification fakes). Closes your #1 named gap. Additive; nothing deploys.

Why Fable 5, not Opus 4.8

The judgment is in the real-vs-mocked split — deciding what's safe to gate on so CI is green and deterministic without secrets. Get that wrong and you get flaky CI, which is precisely the cleanup burden this whole approach avoids.

What it accomplishes

Gives every future agent PR an automatic verification gate — the prerequisite you named for granting agents more autonomy safely.

weight: medium ~4.5M tok · ~$80 open prompt →

3 Meta-System Upgrade — skills standard + one OSS tool

Align the skills system with the SKILL.md open standard and a rulesync-style one-source flow (normalize provenance headers, document the target), and wire in one high-leverage OSS tool end-to-end — default context7 and/or Serena as MCP — demonstrated working. Plan the rest.

Why Fable 5, not Opus 4.8

This is design over your meta-system — how the whole "one source, many wrappers" machine should evolve and which tool pays off first. It rewards a model that reasons well about tradeoffs across the whole stack, not just executes a spec.

What it accomplishes

Stops your skills system being bespoke hand-maintenance, and makes every agent sharper immediately by killing the stale-docs / token-bloat failure modes.

weight: large ~5.5M tok · ~$100 open prompt →

4 App Baseline — bring one app to standard

Take one non-production app (default apps/math-quiz/): add a per-app AGENTS.md, a README/runbook, real tests for the load-bearing logic (run, with output), a health report, and a reusable "baseline checklist" so the mission re-runs on the next app. Bounded to one folder; additive.

Why Fable 5, not Opus 4.8

Judging an unfamiliar app's true state and what its load-bearing logic actually is takes real reasoning. Honest note: this is the most Opus-suitable of the five — once the pattern exists, re-run it on later apps with Opus 4.8 to conserve Fable budget.

What it accomplishes

Turns an under-documented app into a tested, documented baseline — and produces a repeatable checklist that upgrades the rest of the portfolio one app at a time.

weight: medium ~4M tok · ~$70 open prompt →

5 Human-out-of-the-loop Blueprint

Design the staged voice → plan → implement → test → review → gated-deploy pipeline: the stages and gates, the compressed phone-friendly approval-packet format, the per-task-class rollout ladder, and the Randy-vs-EA per-user hook — plus one small, tested increment on the voice-router. Mostly design; nothing deploys.

Why Fable 5, not Opus 4.8

This is the most strategic, ambiguity-heavy design task you have — reasoning from your real constraints (solo, non-coder, AWS-biased, two users) to a safe staged plan. Squarely where Fable's extra capability earns its price; run this one at xhigh.

What it accomplishes

Gives you the concrete blueprint for your end-state vision — plus the reusable approval-packet format that makes every other mission's output actionable from your phone.

weight: large ~5M tok · ~$90 open prompt →
