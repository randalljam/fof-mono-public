StrongDM "Software Factory" + Attractor

A three-engineer team shipping production software under a radical rule: "no human writes code, no human reviews code." Two ideas make that safe: satisfaction testing (end-to-end user-story "scenarios" graded by an LLM and kept outside the codebase so agents can't see or game them) and the Digital Twin Universe (local fakes of external services — Okta, Slack, Jira, Google — so agents self-verify thousands of scenarios per hour). Their orchestration blueprint, Attractor, is open source (Apache-2.0) but deliberately spec-only.

The one-paragraph fit for you

This is the single highest-value practice to adopt for your biggest named gaps — no self-verification, no local fakes, no way for agents to check their own work without you. It maps directly onto your stack: your core/ talks to exactly the external systems worth twinning — S3 (s3_archive.py), Pinecone (vectordb.py), Webflow (webflow_api.py), and your LLM providers (llm.py). Build cheap local fakes of those plus a set of LLM-graded "does this satisfy the user" scenarios stored outside the repo, and your agents can verify themselves at scale. That's the concrete mechanism that makes "Randy doesn't review the code" actually safe — and it's fuel for Fable missions 2 and 5.

01 · Technical

How it works

Satisfaction testing (the acceptance layer)

  • A "scenario" is redefined to mean an end-to-end user story, graded flexibly by an LLM rather than by brittle assertions.
  • "Satisfaction" = across all observed agent trajectories through all scenarios, what fraction likely satisfy the user.
  • Crucially, scenarios are a holdout set stored outside the codebase, where the coding agents cannot see them — mimicking an aggressive external QA team and preventing the agents from gaming the tests.

Digital Twin Universe (the self-verification substrate)

  • Behavior-level clones (local fakes/simulators) of third-party services — StrongDM built twins of Okta, Jira, Slack, Google Docs/Drive/Sheets — replicating APIs, boundary conditions, and observable behaviors.
  • This lets agents self-verify thousands of scenarios per hour with no rate limits, abuse detection, or API cost, entirely outside production. (The CTO's benchmark: "if you haven't spent $1,000 on tokens today per engineer, your factory has room to improve.")

Attractor (the orchestration blueprint)

The OSS repo strongdm/attractor (Apache-2.0) ships only Natural-Language Specs — three markdown files, no application. You build your own by handing a coding agent: "Implement Attractor as described by the repo." Per the spec, Attractor is a DOT-graph pipeline runner: you define a multi-stage AI workflow as a directed graph in Graphviz DOT. Nodes are tasks (LLM call / human gate / conditional / parallel fan-out); edges carry routing conditions. Notable features: a model_stylesheet (CSS-like per-node model/provider/effort selection), goal_gate nodes that must reach SUCCESS before exit, checkpoint-and-resume, human-in-the-loop "Interviewer" gates, and a pluggable codergen backend that can spawn Claude Code / Codex / Gemini CLI in subprocesses. LLM-agnostic by design.

Open source? Attractor is Apache-2.0 but spec-only (a blueprint you implement). The factory itself — the DTU, scenario suites, the production pipeline — is proprietary; only the approach is public.

Why this is the keystone practice

Everything else in your roadmap — the CI floor, the human-out-of-the-loop pipeline, running agents unattended — depends on agents being able to check their own work without you and without hitting production. Satisfaction scenarios (kept outside the repo so agents can't game them) give you the acceptance bar; the digital twins give agents a safe, fast, free place to verify against it. Together they are the mechanism that turns "Randy reviews every PR" into "Randy reviews the ones the scenarios flag." Start small — a couple of twins and ten scenarios for one app — and it compounds.

03 · Caveats

What to keep in mind

It's real engineering to build

Twins and scenario suites don't come for free. Handle: but this is exactly the additive, testable, non-production work your Fable agents are good at — make "build a twin for X" a mission. Start with one dependency.

Attractor is spec-only

You implement it, or just borrow the pattern. Handle: you may not need the full DOT-graph runner — the transferable gold is satisfaction testing + twins. Adopt Attractor's model_stylesheet idea (per-node model routing) if you build orchestration.

The factory is proprietary

Only the approach is public; team size and framing are from secondary sources. Handle: adopt the two named innovations; treat the rest as inspiration, not spec.

04 · Do this

Adopt this (fuel for Fable missions 2 & 5)

1
Twin one external dependency.

Start with the cheapest, highest-value one — a local fake of core/llm.py or a filesystem-backed core/s3_archive.py — so agents can exercise those paths with no cost and no production risk.

2
Write ~10 LLM-graded scenarios for one app, stored outside the repo.

Plain user stories ("a user asks X and gets a cited answer") that an LLM grades for satisfaction. Keep them where the coding agents can't see them, so they can't be gamed.

3
Let agents self-verify against the twins + scenarios.

Wire it into the CI floor (Fable mission 2): the gate isn't just unit tests, it's "do the scenarios still pass against the twins." This is the substrate for running agents unattended (mission 5).

Bottom line: of everything in the OSS discovery, this is the practice that most directly unlocks your end-state — agents that verify themselves so you don't have to read code. Your core/ already isolates every external dependency, which makes twinning unusually easy for you. Build one twin and ten out-of-repo scenarios, wire them into the CI floor, and you've laid the keystone for human-out-of-the-loop. Study Attractor for the orchestration pattern; adopt satisfaction testing + digital twins now.
