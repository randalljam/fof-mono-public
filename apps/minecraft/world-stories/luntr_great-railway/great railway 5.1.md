## Rules & Game Approach

- **Game Mode**
  - Survival **only**.
  - No Creative, no flying, no spawning items.
  - **Difficulty**
  - Normal survival play.
  - Very brief switches to **Peaceful** only when:
    - Helping 5-year-old **TreasureHunterM** with something stressful.
    - Pausing to figure out a mechanic in safety.
- **Cheats & Teleporting**
  - In a previous big survival world, the family **used /teleport and cheats a lot**.
  - In this world:
    - **No teleporting at all**, even when rjcomp (world host) plays alone.
    - No command-based travel or shortcuts; **rails are the main travel system**, so teleporting would make the Great Railway seem meaningless.
- **Playstyle**
  - Family-focused world, shared progress.
  - Exploration, rail-building, and story are more important than pure efficiency.
- **Version and Mod**
  - World name is "Lunter"
  - Minecraft version is Java 1.21.10
  - Using mod Biomes O'Plenty and we may incorporate some of the non-vanilla biomes, plants, blocks, etc. in the story

## Story (Great Railway) – Retrospective Chapters

These chapters are for narration. You can expand each one into a written story in your other thread.

### Chapter 1 – **Spawn on the Broken Line**
- The family appears on a lonely **beach beside the Badlands**.
- They spot an **abandoned mineshaft and a lone minecart** almost immediately.
- Early days are tough:
  - Struggle for food.
  - Survive from **bread** looted from minecart chests (wheat).
- They begin salvaging **rails and wood** from Mineshaft #1.
- They slowly realize this isn’t just a random mineshaft; it feels like part of a **long-lost railway** buried under the red cliffs.
- Discovery that **salmon** in the nearby waters can be farmed:
  - Salmon becomes the main early food.
  - The “Salmon Coast” begins.

### Chapter 2 – **Searching Beyond the Red Hills**
- **PumaJockey** sets out on a long expedition, heading far **west**.
  - She searches for a friendly **green biome** (oak/birch forests, animals, grass).
  - Instead, she finds **only more Badlands**, stretching on and on.
  - No cows, no sheep, no classic forest—just endless red and orange.
- She returns to report that the world is harsher and more broken than expected.
- **rjcomp** explores in another direction, only a few hundred blocks away.
  - Finds **Mineshaft #2** and another **huge cave**.
  - This confirms that there were **multiple lines** of the old railway crisscrossing the Badlands.
- The family decides that instead of searching endlessly for a perfect biome,
  they will **reclaim and rebuild** what’s already here.

### Chapter 3 – **Forging the First True Line**
- The family decides to **link the two key areas**:
  - The **Beach Camp / Original Spawn**.
  - The **Cave Base at Mineshaft #1**.
- First attempt:
  - rjcomp builds a rail line that snakes along the wall of the Badlands.
  - It’s safe-ish, but full of curves and turns.
  - When they ride it, they discover it’s **slow and frustrating**.
- Rebuild:
  - The line is redesigned to be straighter and smoother.
  - This becomes the **first proper section of the Great Railway**.
- The **Cave Base** becomes the main **home base**:
  - Main storage chests are moved there.
  - From here, they delve deep into the caves:
    - Gathering large amounts of **iron and gold**.
    - Eventually finding around **25 diamonds**.
- On another axis from the Beach Camp (across water in the opposite direction from the Cave Base), they discover **Village A**:
  - A small village that will later become one of the first official **stations** in the restored network.
  - The idea of connecting **villages by rail** begins to take shape.

### Chapter 4 – The Glazed Hall of Power

By now, the Great Railway had grown beyond that first shaky line between the Beach Camp and the Cave Base. The family had rails, iron, gold, and diamonds; what they needed next was **power**—the kind that could only come from the strange magic that hummed beneath the world.

So **rjcomp** studied the nearby lava pool and shaped it carefully into a **Nether portal**, frame by frame. When the purple shimmer finally appeared, the family stood at the edge of the unknown. On the other side, they found not gentle rails and salmon waters, but a harsh red underground: basalt, fire, and **piglins**—the moody merchants of the Nether.

To the family, these piglins became like the **old tunnel workers** of legend, still clinging to their gold-based economy. The crew brought their carefully mined **gold** and traded it away, chunk by chunk, in exchange for precious **leather**. It felt strange, turning shining gold into scraps of hide, but everyone knew why: leather meant **books**, and books meant **enchanting**. If the Great Railway was going to be rebuilt, its engineers would need tools worthy of the old legends.

Back in the Overworld, near the Cave Base, **TreasureHunterM** took on a special job. While the others explored and crafted, he planted **row after row of sugar cane** along the water’s edge. It was careful, patient work—just the sort of repetitive, important task that keeps a railway running. The green stalks rose like tiny flags along the shore, silent proof that the family was thinking ahead.

With leather from the Nether and paper from the sugar cane, the crew crafted **books and bookshelves**. From these grew the **Enchanted Heart of the Railway**: a new enchanting room deep in the Badlands. But rjcomp wanted more than a functional chamber. Around the enchantment table, he built a **glazed terracotta room**, using the rich patterns and warm colors of the Badlands clay.

Getting the glazed terracotta to **line up correctly** was its own kind of puzzle. Every block had to be rotated just right for the patterns to meet and swirl properly. It took time, experimentation, and a good bit of stepping back and saying, “Nope, that’s wrong—try again.” But when it finally came together, the family stood in a **beautiful patterned chamber**, where the colors of the Badlands spiraled around the enchanting table like **ancient signal markers** from the old railway days.

They didn’t know the true history, but in their story, the glazed terracotta tiles became **“railway sigils”**—symbols once used by the ancient engineers to guide trains safely through the wildest lands. Now, they wrapped those sigils around the enchantment table, binding old magic to new rails.

Inside this Glazed Hall of Power, **rjcomp** poured experience and lapis into their gear. His **sword, pickaxe, and bow** received strong enchantments, turning him into a true guardian of the line—able to defend the rails from creepers, skeletons, and whatever else lurked in the caves. Slowly, he began to help the others, too: guiding **PumaJockey, WildPetal, and TreasureHunterM** toward their first **enchanted diamond tools**, step by step.

Meanwhile, **PumaJockey** and **WildPetal** felt the pull of the open sky. Between the **Beach Camp** and the **Cave Base**, high on a mesa ridge, they found a spot with a panoramic view of the red cliffs, rivers, and rails below. There, they made a **Mesa Top Camp**: a cozy outpost with beds, chests, and supplies—less a fortress, more a **rest stop for railway workers**. It became their place to **relax, watch sunsets, and look out over the growing tracks**, a reminder that the Great Railway was not just about power and progress, but also about shared quiet moments in a beautiful, harsh land.

By the end of this chapter, the world had changed. The rails were stronger, the tools were sharper, the magic was awake, and the family had carved out places not just to survive, but to **belong**. Chapter 4 has already happened; the Glazed Hall now hums with power, the Mesa Top Camp waits in the wind, and the crew stands ready for their next moves.

### Chapter 5 – New Lines, New Recruits

With the Glazed Hall of Power glowing softly in the Cave Base and the Mesa Top Camp watching over the Badlands below, the Great Railway stood at a crossroads. The early foundations had been laid. Magic hummed in the tools. The Nether was known, if not yet conquered. Now, the railway needed something new:

- **Fresh hands on the old lines.**
- **New tracks reaching out toward new stations.**

For the moment, **PumaJockey** rested at her mesa outpost, or perhaps tended to other duties beyond the world. Her next great task was already written in the family’s plans: she would one day descend into the deep caves with **Fortune** in hand, finally claiming the wealth that lay beneath the rails. Her books were ready; only the journey itself would have to wait.

But the railway could not pause. There was work to be done today.

**Quest 1: TreasureHunterM and the Mineshaft of Many Webs**  
Far from the original Beach Camp and the Cave Base, **rjcomp** had discovered another **abandoned mineshaft**, another fragment of the shattered Great Railway buried under the Badlands. Its tunnels were laced with **rails, cobwebs, wooden supports**, and mysterious minecart chests.

Until now, **TreasureHunterM** had mostly enjoyed riding the rails and planning fun tracks. This time, the story was different. This chapter would be about him becoming not just a passenger, but a **railway worker and scavenger**, helping to reclaim an entire segment of the old line.

Side by side, **rjcomp** and **TreasureHunterM** would venture into this **Mineshaft of Many Webs**:

- Carefully **harvesting rails** for future lines.
- Cutting down **cobwebs** to use for string and resources.
- Grabbing **wood** from the old supports.
- Opening **minecart chests** to see what little treasures the old railway had left behind.

Every rail they picked up would become part of the Great Railway’s future. Every cobweb cleared would open the way for new trains. This wasn’t just gathering materials—it was **initiating TreasureHunterM as a true Engineer of the Line**, giving him a clear, hands-on role in the family’s grand project.

**Quest 2: WildPetal’s Waterline to the Village**

Back near the **Beach Camp**, the family had a dock and an easy **boat route** to a small nearby village. For a while, the water path was enough: hop in a boat, glide across the waves, and climb ashore.

But **WildPetal** saw more than convenience. She saw a gap in the Great Railway.

The small village across the water had already begun to wake up as a potential **station**: a place of Fletchers and Librarians, of trades and books and arrows. The boats were useful, but in a world where the ancient railway was slowly being reborn, a station without a track felt… incomplete.

So **WildPetal** announced her plan: a **simple, flat railway** running from the **Beach Camp** straight along the water’s edge to the **village**, no towering bridge, no fancy supports—just a clean, solid line of rails hugging the shoreline. With **five or six stacks of tracks** already gathered, the family finally had the resources to make it happen.

Her vision was practical and elegant:

- A **low, flat line** almost level with the water.
- Easy to build, easy to extend.
- A clear, undeniable statement:  
  > “This village is officially part of the Great Railway.”

While rjcomp and TreasureHunterM worked underground to reclaim the buried tracks, WildPetal would stand above, placing rails in the sun and wind, watching the line stretch farther and farther along the shore. Each placed piece would turn the once-isolated village into **Village Station A**, a proper stop on the growing network.

Together, these two quests—**the Mineshaft of Many Webs** and **the Shoreline Village Line**—would transform the Great Railway yet again:

- TreasureHunterM would step into his role as a **true track salvager and builder**.
- WildPetal would become the **architect of the next major station connection**.
- The family’s rails would reach deeper underground and farther across the surface, tightening the web of connections that one day would span **Overworld, Nether, and End**.

Chapter 5 is the **next step**, not a memory but a plan: the story you’ll play out this morning. When it’s done, the Great Railway will be richer in rails, stronger in stations, and the younger engineers of the family will be woven even more tightly into its legend.
